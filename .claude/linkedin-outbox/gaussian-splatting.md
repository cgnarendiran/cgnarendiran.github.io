Your phone can turn a hundred photos of a room into a 3D scene you can fly through.

So how do you show a room from a spot where nobody stood? Software used to work out where the walls are and paint the photos onto them. It falls apart on anything fuzzy. A hedge comes out like melted wax.

Picture a man who decorates wedding halls. He arrives at dawn with balloons and helium. By evening there is an arch over a stage. From the front it is a wall of red and gold.

Nobody built a wall. He tied balloons until it looked like one.

That is how 3D scanning works now. The method is called Gaussian splatting. The software scatters millions of soft coloured blobs and nudges each one until the view matches your photos. A garden takes three million blobs.

Walk round the back of the arch and there is nothing solid there. It is something to look at, not somewhere to walk.

I did not expect the best way to store a room to be a pile of smudges.
