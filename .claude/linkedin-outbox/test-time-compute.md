An AI chatbot puts the same effort into "what's the boiling point of water?" as into a maths proof.

Picture a busy clinic where a doctor gets seven minutes per patient. For a sprained ankle, that's plenty. Then someone walks in with a fever that has run three weeks, and nothing else wrong.

So the doctor takes longer, orders a test, reads the result, and picks the next one.

Can an AI work like that? Increasingly, yes. The newer "thinking" models spend longer on a hard question. They write out their working first, or try it many times and keep the best attempt. Given time to think, a small AI can beat a much bigger one, on questions it could already sometimes get right.

The catch is that extra attempts only help if something can tell a right answer from a wrong one. For a maths problem or a piece of code, that check is easy. For summarising a document or replying to an email, there is no answer key, so the gains stall.

My bet is that the next big step comes from building those checks. I wrote about where the extra time goes, and when it stops paying:
