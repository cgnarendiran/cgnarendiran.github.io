For years, voice assistants could type out every word you said. They still could not say one back in your voice.

Why is that so hard? Writing words down and keeping a voice are different jobs.

Picture a concert. A student fills a notebook with the singer's notes, and somebody at the back runs a tape recorder. You can read the notebook. The voice is not in it. The tape has every hair of it (a mile of brown ribbon nobody can read).

To say it back, a voice assistant needs something in between, a notebook you can sing from. Google's answer, a compressor called SoundStream, is dumber than I expected. Cut the sound into thin slices. One pass scribbles a rough version. The next fixes only what the first pass got wrong. After eight passes the scribble is tiny, and it still plays back your voice, echo of the room and all.

The catch is the recordings these systems learned from. Nearly all are English, read aloud in a quiet room. The slide and shake of a singing voice goes first.
