Old Hollywood shot New York in a Burbank studio: painted fronts propped up from behind, with a car park behind every door.

It worked because the camera only ever looked one way. Now the carpenters are gone and a neural network draws the street in real time (Genie 3, the best of them, has no paper).

So how long does a generated street stay put? About ninety seconds. Nothing stores it, so the bakery is there only while the frames containing it sit in the context window. Run past that and nothing crashes, the model just draws a perfectly reasonable shopfront that is not yours.

The catch is that looking right and being right are separate skills. On Physics-IQ, which tests whether a video model knows how liquids behave, the best scored 29.5 out of 100, and that number tells you nothing about how real the footage looks.

What I would bet on is whoever hires the continuity person, the one who checks the coffee cup is still half full in the next shot, because the set builders are already astonishing.
